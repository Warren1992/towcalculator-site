How Heavy Is a U-Haul Car Trailer Really?

U-Haul car trailers are super convenient: rent it, load the vehicle, and go. The problem is that many people underestimate how much weight they’re actually towing — and that’s where setups get unsafe fast.

The key is to stop thinking “tow rating only” and start thinking: empty trailer weight + vehicle weight + tongue weight + payload. This post breaks down the common surprise points.

The empty weight surprise

Unlike lightweight utility trailers, U-Haul car trailers are built very robustly. That strength comes with a penalty: the trailer itself is heavy before you load anything.

Typical “real-world” takeaway:
  • Empty car trailer weight can be around ~2,000+ lb
  • Before you load a car, you’re already towing about a ton

The exact number varies by trailer type/model, but the important point is: it’s not “a few hundred pounds.”

What happens when you load a car

Let’s use a normal example. Many sedans and small SUVs weigh roughly 3,300–4,300 lb. Add that to a heavy car trailer and you can land in a “sounds fine” zone that’s still payload-problematic.

Example loaded setup:
  • Trailer: ~2,200 lb
  • Vehicle being towed: ~3,800 lb
  • Total trailer weight: ~6,000 lb

This is where people get caught: a 6,000 lb trailer might be under a lot of advertised tow ratings — but that’s not the full story.

Tongue weight is the payload killer

Car trailers commonly run around 10–15% tongue weight. That means your “6,000 lb trailer” can put a lot of weight directly onto the tow vehicle.

Estimated tongue weight (6,000 lb trailer):
~600–900 lb added directly to your vehicle’s payload.

That tongue weight counts against:

  • Payload rating (door sticker)
  • Receiver max tongue weight (hitch label)
  • Rear axle and tire capacity
Tip:
The only way to know your real tongue weight is to measure it. A simple tongue weight scale removes guesswork and prevents “it tows fine” surprises.

Why this catches people off guard

A vehicle might advertise a 7,500 lb tow rating, so a 6,000 lb trailer sounds safe. But tow ratings are often based on an almost empty vehicle (one driver, minimal cargo).

In real life you add passengers, coolers, tools, hitch hardware, and then tongue weight — and payload becomes the real limit.
