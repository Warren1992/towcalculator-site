Can a Honda Pilot Tow a Travel Trailer?

The Honda Pilot is a popular three-row SUV for families, road trips, and light outdoor adventures. With a published tow rating that looks reasonable on paper, many owners assume it can easily handle a travel trailer.

In real-world towing, however, the limiting factor is usually not tow rating — it’s payload and tongue weight. This example shows how those numbers play out.

Why payload matters so much on the Honda Pilot

Tow rating assumes an almost empty vehicle. Real family SUVs are rarely empty.

All of the following count against payload:

  • Driver and passengers (often multiple rows)
  • Car seats, strollers, luggage
  • Coolers, pets, and camping gear
  • Tongue weight from the trailer

Once payload is exceeded, stability and braking performance suffer — even if the trailer weight itself looks “within limits.”

Typical tongue weight for travel trailers

Travel trailers usually place about 10–15% of their loaded weight on the hitch.

  • 3,500 lb trailer → ~350–525 lb tongue weight
  • 4,500 lb trailer → ~450–675 lb tongue weight
  • 5,000 lb trailer → ~500–750 lb tongue weight
Tongue weight alone can consume a large portion of a Honda Pilot’s available payload before passengers or cargo are added.

A realistic Honda Pilot towing example

Let’s look at a common family camping setup:

  • Two adults + two kids: 500 lb
  • Cargo (bags, food, gear): 200 lb
  • Hitch hardware: 75 lb
  • Tongue weight (4,000 lb trailer @ 12%): 480 lb

Total payload used: ~1,255 lb

Depending on the exact Pilot trim and payload sticker, this can leave little margin — or exceed limits entirely.

So… can a Honda Pilot tow a travel trailer?

Yes — but only with smaller, lighter travel trailers and careful loading.

The safest Pilot towing setups:

  • Stick to compact or ultralight travel trailers
  • Keep tongue weight conservative
  • Limit cargo inside the vehicle
  • Verify all numbers using the door sticker
