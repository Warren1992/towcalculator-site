Can a Tesla Model 3 Tow a Travel Trailer?

The first check is whether Tesla permits trailer towing on your exact vehicle. The U.S. 2017–2023 Model 3 manual says trailer towing is not supported. The current U.S. Model 3 towing section lists limits for applicable setups, not blanket approval for every car. If the documents for your specific car do not permit towing, do not tow a trailer with it.

Worked example: 2,000 lb loaded trailer

Do not tow this setup. The example puts 240 lb on the hitch, which is 40 lb over the 200 lb limit shown for an applicable setup in the current Tesla manual. This example is only a screening calculation for a vehicle Tesla explicitly rates to tow. A lighter trailer or an aftermarket hitch does not make an unsupported Model 3 eligible. Use the applicable vehicle documents and limits, including the limit for the trailer’s brake setup.

The estimated 800 lb of vehicle load includes the driver, passenger, vehicle cargo, added hitch hardware and tongue weight. Having payload left does not make an over-limit tongue weight acceptable.

Compare the actual loaded trailer weight and measured tongue weight against the limits for both the vehicle and the trailer. Also verify vehicle and axle load limits and brake requirements. If the permitted loading ranges do not overlap, choose a different trailer or tow vehicle. Do not shift cargo rearward just to hit a lower tongue-weight number.

Confirm eligibility before using the calculator

The calculator can help screen a proposed setup, but it cannot certify it. Follow the vehicle and trailer manufacturers’ loading instructions and verify the loaded setup with scales. Plan for shorter driving range while towing.
